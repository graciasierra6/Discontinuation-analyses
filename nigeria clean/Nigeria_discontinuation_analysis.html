Modern-method discontinuation: all reasons, then program-actionable causes

The analysis follows the Ethiopia presentation sequence: validate the DHS all-method table; examine all-reason modern-method discontinuation, including pregnancy intentions and partner disapproval; then narrow to selected and potentially actionable causes. An original-method ending is different from stopping all modern contraceptive use.

From injectable endings to program action

The first alluvial follows observed first-year injectable episodes that ended for a potentially actionable reason and had one calendar month of follow-up. Subnational diagnostic

How much of each zone gap reflects method mix?

For each zone and method, we estimate the survey-weighted share of eligible modern-method episodes and the 12-month competing-risk probability of an ending for a potentially actionable cause. We pool IUD with other modern methods because several separate cells are sparse. For each pair, a symmetric decomposition divides the constructed gap into (1) the difference in episode method shares, averaged over the two zones’ method-specific risks, and (2) the difference in within-method risks, averaged over their method shares. These components add exactly to the constructed gap. This is a descriptive accounting identity, not a causal estimate. The constructed gap can differ from the directly pooled all-modern life table because the method-specific cumulative incidences are combined using episode shares. Confidence intervals use 500 resamples of survey clusters within strata.

For North East versus South East, the constructed gap is +19.3 percentage points. Different method shares account for +3.5 points; differences within methods account for +15.8 points. Sparse method-by-zone cells, especially South East pill and injectable endings, warrant caution.

Nigeria population and mCPR inputs

The observed 2024 baseline pairs Nigeria DHS 2024 all-women mCPR and current-method mix with UN 2024 women aged 15–49. For 2025, we use the UN 2025 population and assume mCPR rises by 0.4 percentage points, holding the DHS method mix fixed. That increment comes from the Track20/FP2030 modeled all-women mCPR change from 13.2% in 2024 to 13.6% in 2025; it is transferred to the DHS baseline, not a relative 0.4% growth rate. For 2026, the Track20-growth scenario adds another 0.4 percentage points to the projected 2025 baseline (13.90%). The UN-growth scenario adds approximately 0.5 percentage points (14.00% at the displayed precision). The UN increment is the change in its modeled all-women modern-use prevalence from 16.0% in 2025 to 16.5% in 2026; we apply that increment to the DHS-anchored baseline, not the UN prevalence level. These are assumptions, not observed 2025 values or published 2026 forecasts. Sensitivity analysis if some users who discontinue switch

These alternative calculations deduct women using a modern method at year end after an actionable ending. This includes direct switchers and later restarters. AFT survival alone does not predict switching.

  • Small samples: 126 of 200 method × cause × duration pathway cells require pooling; 32 pool across methods. Pooling is triggered by fewer than 10 unique episodes, not repeated calendar landmarks. Rare IUD causes have especially limited support (1–11 observed training events per cause). The AFT fits use episodes observed from initiation; the raw DHS-style life tables also retain eligible delayed-entry episodes.
  • Historical patterns may not persist; the 61+ month group uses 37–60 month experience. The AFT model depends on the assumed survival curves and estimates method effects, without demographic adjustment.
  • Modern use at year end includes EC, consistent with baseline mCPR. This differs from excluding EC from the original-method discontinuation cohort.
  • Survey intervals condition on these assumptions and are not estimates of causal program effects. They do not capture model-selection, recall-error or future-program uncertainty.
